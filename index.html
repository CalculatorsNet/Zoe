    What these numbers mean
    Hardest year
    The year with the most days flagged for combined stress. It is the toughest year in this weather record, not a prediction of the next one.
    Days flagged for combined stress
    The model counts hot, dry days and some unusually hot days with unusual weather combinations. It does not confirm crop damage.
    Unusual days
    The share of the hardest year that looked uncommon beside the other days in this record. It is not the chance of a future event.
    Rotation score
    This combines broad crop traits with the hardest-year weather. Lower means less modeled pressure. It is not a yield forecast or loss percentage.
    Bar pairs
    Reference bars show starting crop-trait scores. Hard-year bars apply a weather adjustment. They are not measured average and worst-year field conditions.
    Crop traits
    Water need and heat sensitivity are broad assumptions. The soil-building score blends cover, soil contribution, and nitrogen-fixing traits. A higher score means more of those assumed traits, not measured soil improvement.
    Rotation order
    The four slots show sequence order only. They are not dates or a locally checked planting calendar.
    Livestock feed
    Forage is plant material grown for grazing, hay, or silage. The slider gives feed value more weight; it does not balance a livestock ration.
    How this comparison is made
    1. Build the weather record. The named source above supplies daily weather for one point near the field center, from 2004 through 2024.
    2. Mark difficult days. The model flags three combinations: hot with low recent rain, hot dry air with strong wind, and very wet with freezing temperatures. A flagged day does not confirm crop damage.
    3. Find the hardest year. ZOE selects the year with the most flagged days. A separate check estimates how unusual that year’s daily conditions look beside the full record.
    4. Compare crop orders. Broad crop assumptions for water need, heat tolerance, wet and cold sensitivity, soil contribution, and feed value are scored against that hard year. Your sliders change which priorities matter most in the ranking. A higher soil-building score means more assumed soil-benefit traits, not measured soil improvement.

    These are planning clues, not yield forecasts. Crop traits are broad assumptions, and the app does not know your soil test, planting dates, livestock ration, or market.
